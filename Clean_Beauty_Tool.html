Is Clean Beauty Still Relevant?

As I began researching Is Clean Beauty Still Relevant? for my next article, I also wanted to experiment with another way for readers to experience market research. I imagined an interactive companion to the article.

The Research Brief brings together the wider market context and what different results could mean for brands. The Decision Tree shows how a series of Yes-or-No questions leads to an answer.

With AI assistance, I built the underlying logic behind both experiences. I broke the broad category question into smaller market questions, determined how much each one would influence the overall outlook, and wrote the Yes-or-No rules that produce the Decision Tree result.

TypeSafe.ai’s recent launch of Jev gave the idea a practical next step. Jev is TypeSafe’s first public System One Model. It answers a focused Yes-or-No question with a percentage showing how likely the answer is Yes. A future version could ask Jev the same questions using the same public research and compare its answers with the reader’s.

Path 2 · How the answer is reached

Decision Tree

Test the question

Move the sliders to answer the market questions. The Decision Tree shows whether clean beauty remains relevant and whether it still functions as one clearly defined category.

Useful for

  • Comparing how people interpret the same research
  • Showing which answer changes the conclusion
  • Discussing different views across a team
  • Returning to the question as the research changes

A Jev demonstration I find especially useful

Wikiracing

TypeSafe introduced Wikiracing in its Jev launch post. At each step, Jev chooses one of the links available on the current Wikipedia page until it reaches the target. The path is visible from beginning to end. That same visibility is central to the Decision Tree: every question, answer and rule that shapes the result can be seen.
